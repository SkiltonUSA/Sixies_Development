# Banner Transition

After the center-out opening reveal, the completed Death Star remains visible
for 500 PAL frames, or ten seconds. Four cumulative 4x4 Bayer masks then
restore its ink pixels to the black background. Every phase is generated from
the preserved Death Star source and written directly to bitmap RAM. This avoids
reading KERNAL ROM through the `$e000` overlay after music starts. The shared
star punch routine runs after each phase so stars remain visible throughout the
handoff.

The dithered RetroDNA artwork is generated by
`scripts/generate_retrodna_logo.py` in the same 320x80 hires format used by the
Death Star. Its eight active character rows are written directly and revealed
from top to bottom, with the stars reapplied after each row. Its replacement
artwork contains Death Star panels, trenches, shadows, and highlights inside
the letter faces. The same 4x4 Bayer conversion preserves that texture as pixel
density, while a generated 40x10 color map uses white, three grays, purple,
dark blue, and light blue. Blue cells follow the source artwork's underside and
recessed shadows instead of forming a generic color band; brighter shadow edges
use light blue so they remain visible against black.

RetroDNA remains visible for another 500 PAL frames, then fades through the
same four thresholds. The Death Star palette and ten bitmap rows are restored
top-to-bottom, and the ten-second cycle begins again. A 16-bit counter advanced
by the 50 Hz music IRQ keeps both holds independent of the perspective
plotter's variable main-loop cost.

Six RetroDNA rows occupy the unused `$8800-$8fff` gap. The remaining two rows
and 400-byte color map live at `$c800-$cc0f`, above the transition sprites and
below I/O space. Empty top and bottom rows are implicit, avoiding a second raw
3.2 KB banner.
