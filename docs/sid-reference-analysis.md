# Sixies SID Reference Analysis

This analysis executes each supplied PSID init/play routine and records SID
register activity once per PAL frame. It measures performance behavior without
copying either reference melody or harmony.

## Shared timing grammar

- Both tunes run at 50 Hz and place sequencer events on a six-frame grid.
- Six frames per sixteenth note gives 125 BPM at four sixteenths per beat.
- Attacks stay on that grid; neither performance uses an alternating 5/7 frame
  swing. Syncopation comes from skipped grid positions and 12/18-frame gaps.
- A useful phrase unit is 96 frames: 16 steps or one 4/4 bar.

## Euphoria

- The traced player has a 392-frame introduction followed by a 9,792-frame
  loop (102 bars, 195.84 seconds).
- Voice 1 is a pulse/noise role-switching part. Its dominant attack interval is
  12 frames (428 occurrences), with 18- and 24-frame gaps. Typical gate lengths
  are 10 frames, with three-frame percussion and 22-frame held accents.
- Voice 2 carries the high moving material, alternating triangle and saw. Its
  dominant attack gap is 18 frames (284), then 12 frames (150). Gate lengths
  concentrate at 16 and 10 frames, while pitch modulation continues between
  attacks.
- Voice 3 occupies the lower register in pulse or triangle+pulse. It attacks
  mostly every 12 or 18 frames and most often sustains for 10 frames.
- The filter is active in low-pass mode. Cutoff spans 0-1920, with a median of
  1536; cutoff-change spacing is dominated by alternating 12- and 36-frame
  intervals. Routing/resonance values change between `$00`, `$81`, and `$c1`.
- The most common 96-frame gate pattern for voice 1 is eight attacks spaced 12
  frames apart. Voice 2 commonly uses five attacks spaced 18 frames apart.

## Eternity #1 (intro)

- The full player state repeats after 5,568 frames (58 bars, 111.36 seconds).
- All three voices use three-frame gate spans, creating the characteristic
  clipped, percussive articulation.
- Voice 1 has 604 attacks. The dominant gaps are 6 frames (355) and 12 frames
  (212), and the part repeatedly switches among noise, pulse, and triangle.
- Voice 2 is sparser: 197 attacks with 12-, 24-, 48-, and 96-frame gaps. It is
  mostly pulse but periodically becomes noise percussion.
- Voice 3 has 527 attacks, mostly at 6- or 12-frame spacing. Its pitch changes
  overwhelmingly every two frames (2,498 intervals), identifying the fast
  three-position arpeggio behavior between sequencer attacks.
- No filter movement is present; the timbral motion is produced by waveform,
  gate, pitch, and role changes instead.
- Common 96-frame patterns combine a dense six-frame voice 1 pulse, a sparse
  voice 2 punctuation part, and a voice 3 pattern with attacks at offsets
  `0,6,18,30,36,48,60,72,78,84` after phase normalization.

## Sixies composition mapping

- Tempo and phrase unit: six frames per step, 16 steps per bar, eight bars.
- Lead: measured 12/18-frame cadence, three-frame short notes and nine-frame
  held accents, with pulse/saw/combined waveform changes by section.
- Bass: measured 18-frame movement and ten-frame sustains, alternating triangle
  and saw/triangle+saw colors.
- Third voice: pulse arpeggio updated every two frames, interrupted by one- to
  four-frame noise hats, snares, and accents.
- Filter/PWM: continuous pulse-width movement; low-pass cutoff changes every 12
  frames and follows a slow triangular sweep.
- Melody, bass line, chord sequence, and eight-bar arrangement are original.
